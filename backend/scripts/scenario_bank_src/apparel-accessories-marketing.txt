You are to assume the role of merchandising manager for THREADWORKS, an independent boutique that sells women's and men's clothing and accessories. The store owner (judge) has asked you to recommend a plan to refresh the store's window and floor displays before the fall season.

THREADWORKS has been in a downtown shopping district for nine years. Foot traffic past the store has remained steady, but fewer people are walking in. The owner believes the windows look the same as they did two years ago, and the best-selling jackets are hung in the back of the store where few customers browse. A competitor two doors down recently redesigned its windows and has seen a visible increase in shoppers.

The store's three full-time employees and two part-time workers have never received training on how to build displays. The owner's last attempt, a pile of sweaters on a table by the door, was rearranged by customers within an hour. Weekend sales account for about 55 percent of weekly revenue, and the owner knows that most shoppers decide within a few seconds whether to walk in. The store's lighting is also dim in the front third of the sales floor, and two display fixtures are damaged.

The store owner (judge) has a budget of $3,500 for the project and wants to complete it within three weeks. The recommendation should include:
- A concept for the fall window display and how it will attract passersby
- A plan for rearranging the sales floor so key products are easy to find
- How staff should greet and assist customers who enter because of the new displays
- A simple way to promote the new look and measure whether it works

You will present your recommendation to the store owner (judge) in a role-play to take place in the store's office. The store owner (judge) will begin the role-play by greeting you and asking to hear your ideas. After you have presented your recommendation and have answered the store owner's (judge's) questions, the store owner (judge) will conclude the role-play by thanking you for your work.
QUESTIONS:
- How would you decide which products belong in the front of the store?
- What would you do if the new displays attract more visitors but do not increase sales?
- How often should the displays change?
HOOK: A boutique's neighbor redesigned its windows and the crowds followed.
